# 2026 Programme Guide — Mobility projects for youth workers (excerpt)

Source: Erasmus+ Programme Guide 2026, English, Version 1 (12/11/2025). Public document.  
This is the **canonical action definition** for the current corpus. Full PDF stays local (`guide/programme-guide-2026_en.pdf`, ~pp. 165–174). Do not rely on the model’s memory of older KA153 wording — the Guide is the source of truth and updates yearly.

---

## What the Action is for

Organisations get support for **professional development and capacity building of youth workers and their organisations** through non-formal/informal learning in mobility.

Aims:

- Educational and professional development of youth workers → better individual practice **and** stronger organisations/systems
- A community of youth workers that raises quality of work with young people
- **Clear impact on participants’ regular work with young people** and on local youth-work practice

Policy hooks (use only when the project actually contributes): EU Youth Strategy 2019–2027, European Youth Goals, European Youth Work Agenda, Youth Dialogue, inclusion/green/digital/participation.

---

## Eligible activity types (high level)

**Professional development activities (PDAs):** study visits, job shadowing, exchanges, peer learning, networking, training courses, seminars/workshops.

**Not eligible:** academic study trips, for-profit activities, tourism, festivals, holiday travel, performance tours, statutory meetings.

**System development and outreach:** optional complementary work that goes beyond normal dissemination (tools, practices, “youth work lab”).

**Preparatory visits:** in a receiving-organisation country, before the PDA; representatives of participating orgs, trainers, facilitators. A **detailed timetable by day and session must be annexed** for every PDA, preparatory visit, and system-development activity.

Minimum PDA duration: **2 consecutive days to 60 days** excluding travel. At least two organisations from different countries. Participants resident in sending/receiving country. Up to 50 participants per activity (trainers/facilitators not counted as participants). Host-country participants must be involved.

## Award criteria (100 points)

To be considered for award:

- **At least 60 / 100**, and
- **At least half** of the maximum on **each** criterion

### Relevance, rationale and impact — max 30 (need ≥ 15)

Among other points, the extent to which:

- Applicant profile is relevant to **youth work**
- Project addresses Youth Dialogue priorities or Youth Goals
- Contributes to inclusion, green, digital, participatory dimensions
- **Relevant to the objectives of this Action**
- Addresses development needs of **participating youth workers and youth-work organisations**
- Reinforces organisational practice (quality, innovation, recognition)
- High-quality learning outcomes for youth workers
- Involves people **active in youth work in the participating organisations** and orgs that do regular local work with young people
- Impact on workers and orgs during and after the project; realistic wider impact
- Newcomers/less experienced orgs introduced to the Action (positive, not sufficient alone)

### Quality of project design and implementation — max 40 (need ≥ 20)

- Consistency between **needs, objectives, participant profiles, and activities**
- Clear preparation / implementation / follow-up; participants involved in all phases
- Balanced countries and gender
- Selection of youth workers appropriate and in line with the legal definition
- Accessible and inclusive activities
- Sustainable / environmentally friendly practices
- Appropriate non-formal (and digital) methods
- Reflection, documentation of learning, Youthpass
- System-development tools replicable if used

### Quality of project management — max 30 (need ≥ 15)

- Practical arrangements and support
- Safety and protection of participants
- Tasks and responsibilities vs Erasmus quality standards
- Cooperation and communication among orgs and stakeholders
- Evaluation of phases and outcomes against objectives
- Dissemination inside and outside participating organisations
- Sustainability of results beyond the project lifetime
