# Pass-rate rules (English) — KA153 / Mobility of youth workers

Derived from 2026 Programme Guide award criteria and five National Agency quality assessments (internal IDs EX-2024-A … EX-2025-E).  
No organisation names or project codes.

Use these as **hard constraints** on chat and generated application sections. Prefer concrete, reviewable wording. Avoid generic AI filler and buzzword stacking.

Threshold reminder: **≥ 60/100** and **≥ 50% of each criterion** (Relevance 30, Design 40, Management 30).

---

## Always

1. **Right beneficiary.** The project is for **youth workers** and **capacity of participating organisations**, with a clear effect on their regular work with young people. Young people are the eventual beneficiaries of better youth work — not the primary participants of a KA153 training course.
2. **Action fit.** State how the proposal meets Mobility of youth workers objectives: professional development, quality youth work, organisational capacity — not tourism, festivals, academic study, or personal wellness as the main aim.
3. **Needs → objectives → activities → learning outcomes → impact** must be one chain. Same numbers, same title, same objectives in every section.
4. **Needs analysis** must show method and evidence **per participating organisation** (surveys, focus groups, dates, sample size, what youth workers actually said). Cite sources if studies are mentioned.
5. **Participants** are staff/volunteers of the consortium, selected with published criteria and weightings, in line with the legal definition of youth worker. Not an open call to the general community as if it were a youth exchange.
6. **Learning outcomes** as knowledge / skills / attitudes that youth workers will use with young people. Youthpass and reflection are planned, not named in passing.
7. **SMART objectives** tied to the identified worker/org needs — not a laundry list of wishes.
8. **Fewer opportunities:** name barriers, adaptation of the programme, and support measures — not a declaration that “inclusion is important.”
9. **All phases:** preparation, implementation, follow-up — with who does what, how participants are involved, and how each phase is evaluated.
10. **Annexes:** a **detailed timetable by day and session** for every Professional Development Activity, preparatory visit (APV), and system-development activity. If an APV has no session programme, do not include it (reviewers will not fund it).
11. **Venue** in a country of a participating organisation, with a reasoned choice (not unexplained travel to a partner country while the applicant sits elsewhere).
12. **Partnership:** why these partners, selection criteria, capacity to each send the stated number of participants, clear roles (sending/receiving/coordinator).
13. **Management:** tasks, safety/insurance, communication tools, result indicators that match the real scale of the project, dissemination **with** target groups, timeline, and indicators.
14. **Sustainability** refers to **this** project’s results, not leftover text from another application.
15. **EU values, Youth Goals / Youth Dialogue, horizontal priorities** (inclusion, green, digital, participation) only where the activities actually deliver them.

---

## Never

1. Never write a KA153 as if youth (or “young entrepreneurs”) were the course participants opening startups, writing business plans, or doing personal dopamine detox as the core outcome.
2. Never leave **internal contradictions**: wrong title, leftover theme (e.g. filmmaking in a digital-literacy draft), changing participant counts, partner counts, or objectives between sections.
3. Never use a **generic** needs analysis (“young people use social media”) without worker-level and organisation-level evidence.
4. Never list learning results that belong to another profession (video production as an end in itself, graphic design, “reduce dopamine dependence”) unless they are clearly tools for youth-work practice.
5. Never claim impact far beyond the size of a short training course.
6. Never treat inclusion, digital, or green as slogans without measures.
7. Never omit partner/participant selection criteria or pretend newcomers automatically prove quality.
8. Never request an APV without a day-by-day session plan annexed.
9. Never copy phrasing that sounds like a template: “various workshops, seminars and surveys” with no detail.
10. Never mix Youth Exchange logic (young people as participants) into a youth-worker mobility.

## Failure modes seen in this corpus (use as negative examples)

| ID | Score | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| EX-2024-A | 55 | Weak needs method; some learning results not relevant to youth work; participant profile unclear (open community vs consortium staff); activities skewed to blogging/content production rather than worker competences |
| EX-2024-B | 41 | Needs centred on young entrepreneurs, not youth workers; logic break (a TC cannot turn workers into startup consultants); venue poorly justified; dissemination untimed / no indicators |
| EX-2024-C | 45 | Horizontal priority unclear; activities = personal wellbeing, not professional development of workers; needs weakly evidenced; learning outcomes not K/S/A |
| EX-2025-D | 62 | Near threshold but: leftover title/theme from another project; generic needs; vague selection and fewer-opportunities measures; **APV without detailed programme**; no dissemination plan; sustainability text from another project |
| EX-2025-E | 52 | Incoherent aim/objectives across sections; needs–objectives–activities–results broken; overstated impact; APV programme missing; prep/follow-up thin; inclusion vague |

Rewrite quality is not enough: EX-2024-C → EX-2025-E only moved 45 → 52.

---

## Anti-AI-trace (from NA-style rejection patterns)

- Prefer specific numbers, places, methods, and named activity formats (non-formal: workshops, simulations, peer learning) over adjectives (“innovative,” “holistic,” “transformative”).
- Do not stack Erasmus keywords without a sentence that shows how the activity meets the criterion.
- Keep one project identity: one title, one theme, one participant table, one objective list.
